# Practical legal pathways for a NSW-based micronational association

**Research note — not legal advice, not an application or certificate of incorporation.**

Parristan may operate as a voluntary, unincorporated civic association as people choose to organise. An alternative, after sufficient members exist, is to investigate registering a NSW incorporated association for governance and contracting. An incorporated association is an Australian legal entity, **not** a sovereign state.

Under NSW rules, an incorporated association generally requires **at least five members**, along with committee, public-officer and constitutional arrangements. Its registration and financial reporting do not grant sovereignty, jurisdiction, tax exemptions or citizenship recognised by Australia.

Sources:
- [NSW Government — About incorporated associations](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/incorporated-associations/about-incorporated-associations)
- [Service NSW — Apply to register an incorporated association](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/apply-to-register-an-incorporated-association)
- [NSW Government — Running an incorporated association](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/incorporated-associations/running-an-incorporated-association)

## Evidence checklist

- [ ] A real group of willing participants has formed.
- [ ] The group has agreed on its purposes, decision rules and financial controls.
- [ ] The constitutional document has been checked against any relevant incorporation requirements.
- [ ] Legal advice has been obtained before undertaking territorial or government-recognition claims.
- [ ] Premises or land-use agreements are documented and lawful.

There is **no guaranteed legal route** from micronational membership to independent statehood. For wider international-law context see the [Montevideo Convention, Article 1](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1933v04/d134).
