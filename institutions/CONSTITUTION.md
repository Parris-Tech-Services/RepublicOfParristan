## Preamble

We, the members of the Republic of Parristan, associate voluntarily to form a peaceful democratic micronational community committed to human dignity, liberty, accountability and the common good.

## Article I — Nature and purposes

1. Parristan is a voluntary civic and micronational association aspiring to organise as a democratic republic.
2. Parristan may aspire to international recognition; it shall never misrepresent recognition, jurisdiction or sovereignty.
3. Its internal rules bind members only through lawful and voluntary consent, subject to applicable law.

## Article II — Fundamental rights

1. Every member enjoys equal treatment under this constitution without discrimination.
2. Members have freedom of thought, belief, expression, peaceful association and participation in government, consistent with the rights of others and applicable law.
3. Membership and officeholding may be ended voluntarily.
4. No officer may demand passports, government identity documents or private financial information through public channels.
5. A member accused of violating internal rules is entitled to notice, an opportunity to respond and a fair decision.

## Article III — Membership and citizenship

1. The term “citizen” denotes a member of Parristan; it does not grant a legal nationality or immigration status.
2. Admission, suspension and removal shall be governed by a separate published policy and subject to appeal.
3. Members may not be charged compulsory taxes or fees without a constitutionally authorised, disclosed and lawful decision.
4. A membership register must respect privacy and applicable data protection requirements.

## Article IV — The Assembly

1. The Assembly consists of all eligible adult citizen-members, each with one vote.
2. It elects officers, approves budgets and treaties, and makes internal rules.
3. Ordinary motions pass by a simple majority of valid votes cast, subject to a quorum defined in standing orders.
4. Assembly records, vote totals and decisions are public; individual ballots are secret when appropriate.
5. Until the first election, an interim convenor may facilitate meetings but shall not exercise permanent constitutional powers.

## Article V — Executive government

1. The Head of State is the President, elected by eligible members for a two-year term.
2. The President represents the association and implements Assembly resolutions.
3. Expenditure, contracts and public representations must remain within the approved budget and delegated authority.
4. The President may be removed for serious misconduct by a two-thirds vote of the Assembly after notice and a fair hearing.
5. If the Presidency is vacant, the Assembly appoints a temporary acting convenor until an election is held.

## Article VI — Elections and accountability

1. Elections are open, regular and conducted under published neutral rules.
2. Candidates have equal access to the election process.
3. An electoral officer who is not a candidate should administer the poll wherever membership allows.
4. Financial accounts and material conflicts of interest must be disclosed to members.
5. No office may be held indefinitely merely by founding status.

## Article VII — Justice and dispute resolution

1. A three-person Ethics and Appeals Panel may be chosen by the Assembly when sufficient eligible members exist.
2. Panel members must recuse themselves from conflicts of interest.
3. Decisions are internal membership decisions only: the panel is **not** a court of Australian law and has no power to imprison, fine under public law or seize property.
4. Members retain all remedies available under applicable law.

## Article VIII — External relations

1. Official correspondence may be authorised by Assembly resolution.
2. No document shall falsely assert diplomatic status, consular privilege, government accreditation or recognition.
3. Any proposed agreement must identify the parties, their authority and the agreement's actual legal or nonbinding character.

## Article IX — Territory, property and physical premises

1. No territorial sovereignty, property title, exclusive jurisdiction or right of entry arises from this constitution.
2. Any physical premises or land use requires genuine owner consent and compliance with applicable law.
3. Members cannot pledge or transfer property belonging to another person.
4. The Assembly must disclose and evaluate any proposed territorial claim before adopting an official position.

## Article X — Supremacy of applicable law

1. Australian and all other applicable laws take precedence where they conflict with Parristan's internal rules.
2. The association does not purport to grant tax exemptions, exemptions from planning law, immigration status, passports recognised for travel, or immunity from legal proceedings.

## Article XI — Amendments

1. Any citizen may propose an amendment with its full text publicly available before voting.
2. An amendment requires at least 14 days' notice and a two-thirds majority of valid votes cast, with quorum as defined by the Assembly.
3. The amendment record must include the vote result, effective date and version history.
4. Fundamental rights and democratic participation cannot be permanently suspended by executive order.

## Article XII — Ratification and transition

1. This constitution takes effect only after adoption by an appropriately convened founding assembly.
2. The founding meeting must publish eligibility rules, a quorum and verifiable vote totals beforehand.
3. Within 90 days after ratification, the first election should take place.
4. Until ratified, none of these articles confer office or authority on anyone.
