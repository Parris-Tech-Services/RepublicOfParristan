# Open Source Act 2026 — Consolidated Text (proposal)

**Republic of Parristan · 19 November 2026 · Status: PROPOSED. Not adopted.** This is an editorial consolidation of two previously published draft texts. The [founding-assembly minutes](FOUNDING_ASSEMBLY_2026-11-19.md) record unanimous adoption of an open-source *strategy* and activation of the OSPO charter, but do **not** establish passage of either full Open Source Act text. The assembly has not recorded an adoption vote on this consolidated bill. Its provisions, including hosting, spending, exemptions and future appointments, have no operative force unless adopted through the appropriate procedure.

**Provenance:** Salvaged and revised from [PR #13](https://github.com/Parris-Tech-Services/RepublicOfParristan/pull/13), which proposed recording two contradictory Acts as enacted and reused a Gazette number now occupied by another valid notice. Neither claim is carried forward here.

## Where the two draft texts differ

| Point | [Governance version](OPEN_SOURCE_ACT_DRAFT.md) (Part 1) | [Operational pack version](../operational-pack/government/open-source-act.md) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open source rule | Required, with written, public exemptions that expire after 12 months | "Wherever a workable option exists"; the Office approves exceptions in writing |
| Contractors | Must follow the Act; code written for the Republic is published | Not covered |
| Formats | Open formats; yearly export test | ODF, plain text, PNG, SVG, CSV, JSON |
| Who appoints the CDO | Not specified in the Act text (the Assembly did it on 19 Nov) | The President |
| Where the Office sits | Stands alone | Inside a Ministry that is not yet adopted |
| Hosting | Not stated in the Act | Republic-owned equipment (The Shed), backups on Republic-owned storage |
| Privacy | Open source is no substitute for security; member records stay private | No publishing of addresses, phone numbers or emails without consent |
| Review | After 12 months | None |

## Recommendation

For consideration, use the **governance draft as the base**. It has exemptions with expiry dates, covers contractors, tests data export once a year, and has a review date, which are the pieces that stop a small group depending on one person. Consider three useful elements from the pack draft: the plain list of formats, data-hosting controls, and the consent rule for personal details. Resolve future appointment rules by proposing that the Assembly appoints the CDO until a President is elected, and that subsequent appointments are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Assembly. The Office should not sit inside a Ministry until the Ministries are adopted.

## Proposed text

**1. Short title.** Open Source Act 2026.

**2. Purpose.** To keep the Republic's administration under its own control: auditable software, portable data and no forced dependence on a single vendor. The Act follows the French Gendarmerie (GendBuntu) and Schleswig-Holstein examples.

**3. Open source by default.** National systems must run on open source software (OSI-approved licence). Anything else needs a written exemption under section 6.

**4. Open formats.** Official records are created, stored and published in open formats: ODF (`.odt`, `.ods`, `.odp`), plain text or Markdown for documents; PNG or SVG for images; CSV or JSON for data; PDF/A for archives. Every national system must be able to export its data in an open format, tested at least once a year.

**5. Office of Sovereign Systems.** The Office runs this Act and reports to each Assembly. It is led by a Chief Digital Officer. The Assembly appoints the CDO. Once a President is elected, the President nominates the CDO and the Assembly confirms. The first CDO is the person appointed on 19 November 2026.

**6. Exemptions.** The Office may grant a written exemption where no suitable open source option exists. Each states the reason, the alternatives considered, an exit plan and an expiry no later than 12 months out. Exemptions are listed in a public register that contains no personal data.

**7. Contractors.** Anyone who builds or runs a national system for the Republic must meet sections 3 and 4. Software written for the Republic under contract is released under an OSI-approved licence unless the Office records an exemption. No contract may block the Republic from exporting its own data.

**8. Data stays home.** National data is hosted on equipment owned or controlled by the Republic or its citizens, with backups kept on Republic-owned storage in a different location. A managed service may be used only with a recorded exemption.

**9. Privacy and security.** No national system publishes a citizen's address, phone number or personal email without written consent. Membership, identity, election and financial records stay access-controlled. National systems must have multi-factor authentication, patching, encrypted backups and a tested restore.

**10. Existing systems.** Within 90 days of commencement the Office publishes an inventory and a migration plan for any non-compliant system.

**11. Review.** The Assembly reviews this Act within 12 months.

**12. Limits.** This Act governs the Republic's own administration as a voluntary association. It does not create legal sovereignty or override Australian law.

**13. Commencement and supersession.** If adopted by the Assembly, this Act would commence on the effective date specified in that resolution and replace the two previous *draft proposals* as the intended working text. Any existing valid separate resolutions, including the OSPO charter, should be reconciled expressly; they are not repealed by merely publishing this proposal.
