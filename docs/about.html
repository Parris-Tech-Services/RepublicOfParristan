About the Republic

A community, a constitution, a common purpose.

Parristan is a serious effort to create a peaceful, voluntary and democratic micronational community — with accountable institutions and truthful public records.

Why create Parristan?

A micronation can be a genuine civic and cultural undertaking even before it has any prospect of legal statehood. Parristan's founding aims are to bring together real people, protect individual dignity, make decisions democratically and create a lasting culture of service and responsibility.

Our ratified internal Constitution

The founding assembly of all five members unanimously adopted the internal Constitution on 19 November 2026 (5 YES, 0 NO, 0 ABSTAIN), as recorded in the minutes supplied by the organiser. The consolidated copy currently reproduces the previously published v0.1 draft; the exact voted-on version needs confirmation. The President must still be elected.

What statehood would involve

Article 1 of the 1933 Montevideo Convention identifies a permanent population, defined territory, government and capacity to enter relations with other states. These are widely cited statehood characteristics, not a recipe that guarantees sovereignty or diplomatic recognition.

Present legal position: Parristan is not recognised as an independent country. No territorial sovereignty, legally recognised nationality or diplomatic recognition has been substantiated. The association's first President, Joshua Parris, was confirmed by its members on 19 November 2026; this is an internal office, not a public one. The voluntary association did appoint Glenn as its Chief Digital Officer. Members in Australia remain subject to Australian law.

Building the institution before the title

The founding group and 5–0 constitutional vote are now recorded. The first President was confirmed 5–0 on 19 November 2026. Next is creating privacy-respecting membership administration and practical digital services. No national symbols were adopted by the founding minutes.

Publication here is for transparency, not a claim of public-law authority. Read the legal and territorial position and development roadmap.
