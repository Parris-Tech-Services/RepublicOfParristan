# Legal and Territorial Position

**Status: 19 November 2026.** This document distinguishes Parristan's aspirations from legal facts.

## Present status

Parristan is an emerging micronational project. It has **not** established internationally recognised sovereignty, a recognised nationality or extraterritorial legal authority. Organising a micronation is different from creating a state recognised under international or Australian law.

## Montevideo framework

Article 1 of the 1933 Montevideo Convention identifies four widely discussed characteristics of statehood:

- Permanent population
- Defined territory
- Government
- Capacity to enter into relations with other states

Meeting a checklist is not a guarantee of international recognition. Legal scholarship also debates declarative and constitutive approaches to statehood, and effective statehood is influenced by political and legal context.

Primary historical reference: [1933 Convention text via the US Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1933v04/d134).

## Territory

No land parcel, waterway, building, public area, airspace or online resource is presently claimed as sovereign Parristan territory. A repository and its hosting environment are platforms for records, not geographic territory.

Any later territorial proposal must document:

1. Identifiable location and boundaries.
2. Property ownership and owner consent.
3. Applicable planning, land-use and other law.
4. Whether a claim is merely symbolic, contractual or intended as a sovereignty claim.
5. Independent legal review before a public assertion.

No trespass, squatting, adverse possession scheme or unilateral occupation is contemplated.

## Position in Australia

Members physically in Australia remain subject to Australian federal, state and local laws, including tax, criminal, property and planning law. A proclamation alone does not change those obligations. Parristan does not claim authority to issue legally recognised passports or grant immunity.

## Recognition and diplomacy

Correspondence with a micronation, government agency or diplomat is **not evidence of recognition** unless the respondent expressly makes an authoritative recognition decision. Every claimed recognition must link to a verifiable primary record.

## Public statements policy

Use the terms “micronation”, “aspiring republic”, “membership”, and “proposed constitution” accurately. Do not claim sovereign status or a diplomatic office as a fact until independently substantiated.
