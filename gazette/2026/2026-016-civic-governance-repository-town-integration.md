## Preamble — governing principle

The Republic of Parristan adopts a lawful, realistic and democratic framework for integrating its fictional settlements and simulated residents into the shared 3D world of Repository Town, NSW:

**Parristan adapts to the laws, geography and institutions of the world in which its members live. The real world does not bend to Parristan's fictional institutions.**

The project builds a living virtual society while maintaining truthful records, voluntary membership, respect for Australian law and democratic accountability.

## Text approved as an ordinary motion (clauses 1–8, 10–11)

### 1. Australian law and lawful civic status

Parristan continues as a voluntary civic and micronational association. Australian Commonwealth, NSW and applicable local laws take precedence. Parristan possesses no Australian governmental jurisdiction, recognised sovereign territory or legal authority over non-members. No fictional map, proclamation or game mechanic creates ownership, public office, planning approval or right of entry. Real-world activity requires lawful permissions and relevant owner consent.

### 2. Separation of real and simulated citizenship

**Real civic membership:** human members admitted through adopted membership procedures and holding the constitutional rights and obligations of members.

**Simulated population:** AI-generated fictional residents of Repository Town's game world. They may have fictional identities, families, occupations, finances, relationships and community roles, but are **not** real members, Assembly voters, officeholders or recognised national citizens.

### 3. Integration into Repository Town

The Assembly endorses in principle one fictional shared 3D world including Repository Town, the real-world locality represented by Narromine, and Parristan settlements including Lantern Point and Tealford. Preserve Repository Town's existing geography and features; represent Lantern Point south of Repository Town at approximately Narromine's represented distance; place Tealford and other towns within a consistent documented *fictional* coordinate system. Compressed playable travel is permitted when represented distances remain intelligible. No game marker, boundary, clearance distance or proximity to Tomingley constitutes a territorial, ownership or land-access claim over real NSW land.

### 4. Local civic associations

Create **fictional Local Civic Associations** in the simulation, beginning with Tealford, for community halls and public space, volunteers and community assistance, simulated postal and communications services, markets, local events, maintenance and improvement proposals. Any corresponding real-world chapter is a voluntary association of consenting human members and receives no Australian local-government authority.

### 5. Civic Service (Simulation)

Within the game, a clearly fictional **Parristan Civic Service** may simulate public safety, traffic incidents, lost property, peaceful dispute resolution and community assistance. It has no power to arrest, detain, search, seize property, impose actual fines or interfere with real NSW Police or emergency services. Game courts, police and penalties have no effect outside the simulation.

### 6. Finances, dues and simulated rates

Separate real association finances from fictional currency and budgets. AI residents and businesses may pay simulated rates, wages, rent, taxes and service charges *in-game only*. No simulated obligation creates a real debt or binds Australian land. Real member contributions remain voluntary unless separately and validly authorised under the Constitution and applicable law; no compulsory real taxation, land rates, public-law penalties or new coercive power is created. All real spending remains subject to existing Assembly financial controls and budgets.

### 7. Realistic and consistent simulation

Adopt consistent technical design standards across settlements, progressing towards:

1. NSW-inspired believable roads, intersections, markings, shoulders, footpaths and traffic.
2. Repository Town-quality NPC appearance and animation.
3. Persistent households, families, jobs and homes.
4. Functioning shops, workplaces, community facilities and economic consequences.
5. Connected transport and vehicle travel between settlements.
6. Usable entries and actual interiors where expressly implemented.
7. Credible landscape, terrain, weather, lighting and ordinary routines.
8. Meaningful simulated finances and local services.
9. Reliable mobile and desktop performance.
10. Testing and live-deployment verification before release.

Do not label unfinished game functions as operational.

### 8. Governance and decision-making

Continue the adopted Constitution and Gazette 2026-009 standing procedures. Ordinary motions pass by the defined quorum and simple majority of valid votes cast, with a 48-hour written voting window that can close early once all eligible members vote. Constitutional amendments remain subject to Article XI's full-text publication, **at least 14 days' notice**, two-thirds approval and quorum; the founding-assembly one-hour procedure does not create an exception.

### 10. Implementation and verification

Authorise the President and designated technical contributors, within existing constitutional and financial authority, to develop and test the shared fictional-world integration in stages:

1. Finish, test and verify Lantern Point.
2. Integrate and test Tealford against established shared-world infrastructure.
3. Reuse the verified systems for the remaining fictional settlements.
4. Improve households, transport, economy and community institutions.

Test before merging into main; distinguish verified implementations, deployments and unresolved limitations.

### 11. Transparency and official records

The Secretary shall record the exact motion, eligible electorate, quorum, YES/NO/ABSTAIN vote totals, any separate amendments, and certified minutes. The Gazette shall distinguish adopted ordinary motions, proposed constitutional amendments, simulation rules and Australian legal obligations. No unrecorded vote or approval shall be represented as having occurred.

## Clause 9 — proposed Article XIII, publication and consultation **only**

The Assembly endorsed publishing this **separate future constitutional amendment proposal**, **not adopting Article XIII on 23 November**:

> **Proposed Article XIII — Civic and Simulated Services**
>
> **XIII.1 — Direction of adaptation.** Parristan's internal rules shall adapt to applicable Australian law and the lawful institutions of the places in which its members reside.
>
> **XIII.2 — Separation of worlds.** Fictional territories, simulated citizens, governments, courts, enforcement services and finances shall have no real-world public-law authority or legal effect.
>
> **XIII.3 — Voluntary civic services.** Parristan may organise lawful voluntary member associations, chapters and services, subject to consent, privacy protections, approved financial controls and applicable law.
>
> **XIII.4 — Preservation of rights.** Nothing in this Article overrides existing constitutional rights, confers sovereign or police powers, authorises compulsory land charges or reduces the protections governing constitutional amendments.

A distinct Article XI vote can occur **only after at least 14 days' notice of the fixed full amendment text** and confirmation of quorum and votes. No vote on the constitutional amendment is recorded here; a proposed future date alone is not a duly given notice. See the full text above for the notice-ready proposal.

## Commencement and limits

Subject to the constitutional eligibility and adopted voting procedures, clauses **1–8 and 10–11** have internal effect from the reported unanimous ordinary vote on **23 November 2026**, within the association's existing authority. Clause **9** authorises *publication and consultation only*. No real-world government powers, territorial claims, compulsory debts, amendments to the Constitution or unapproved expenditures result from this motion.
