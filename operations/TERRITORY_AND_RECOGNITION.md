# Territorial, Legal-Statehood and Recognition Roadmap

**Prepared:** 19 November 2026 · **Status:** research and implementation pathways; no sovereign territorial claim or recognition has been achieved.

## A. Three different concepts

1. **Civic premises** — a real room, meeting venue or lawful lease. A useful operational home, **not** a sovereign territory.
2. **Land ownership or lawful tenure** — property rights under the existing jurisdiction. Buying/owning land **does not** create independent sovereignty.
3. **Internationally sovereign territory** — a very different legal and political status, not produced by website declarations, land purchase or private lease agreements.

## B. Defined territory study

A future factual proposal would require an identified site, boundaries, land-title searches, consent of landowners, relevant public land and land-use rules, access and environmental conditions, services, finance, safety and independent legal advice.

**Current record:** no sovereign parcel or border is adopted. Do not enter, mark, occupy or claim property without lawful consent. A virtual community can operate without claiming a physical state.

## C. Statehood research

The [1933 Montevideo Convention](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1933v04/d134) identifies population, defined territory, government and capacity to enter relations with other states as widely cited statehood criteria. It is not a simple application form or an automatic path to recognition. Recognition has political and legal dimensions, and national governments need not accept a unilateral declaration.

| Element | Evidence presently available | Outstanding real-world proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permanent population | Five founding members reported agreed | Actual continuous residential population connected to a defined territory |
| Defined territory | None claimed | Substantiated boundaries and lawful international status |
| Government | Draft constitutional architecture | Institutions actually ratified, elected and exercising effective authority |
| External relations | Diplomatic policy drafts | Verifiable institutional capability and real external relationships |

## D. Honest external relations

- Establish an authorised, accountable correspondent after the Assembly adopts governance.
- Distinguish acknowledgement of correspondence from recognition of a state.
- Use an evidence register with exact original response, date, sender, capacity and the claim it does or does not support.
- Do not represent a citizen as an accredited ambassador, diplomat or consul without the relevant recognised state's authority.
- Any proposed partnership should respect the relevant actual governments, persons and laws.

## E. Actionable stages

1. Form constitutional government and a lawful administrative entity where useful.
2. Establish a legal meeting venue and regular civic activity.
3. Obtain an independent legal opinion before any serious claim of territorial jurisdiction or statehood.
4. Build genuine cultural and cooperative relationships and document actual outcomes.
5. Only make formal recognition approaches when authorised, well-supported and clearly identifying Parristan's current position.
6. Publish source-backed updates. **Do not mark sovereignty achieved unless independently verifiable facts justify it.**

## Not an Australian-law exemption

Australia's Commonwealth, state and local laws continue to apply to members in Australia. Parristan does not grant valid travel passports, visas, legal immunity or exemptions from tax. Neither NSW incorporation nor a land purchase changes this.

A sovereign-state outcome depends on facts and external actors outside this repository and outside the founding members' unilateral control.
