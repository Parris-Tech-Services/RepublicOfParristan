# Republic of Parristan

**A developing micronation with a serious commitment to democratic self-government, lawful organisation and peaceful diplomacy.**

> **Status (19 November 2026):** Founding initiative established; constitution proposed but not yet ratified. Parristan is a self-proclaimed micronational project, **not presently a recognised sovereign state**. Its institutions and membership will be real, voluntary and accountable. Claims of legal independence, territorial jurisdiction or diplomatic recognition will not be made without evidence.

## Our purpose

The Republic of Parristan aims to build and sustain a real micronational community: people who adopt a constitution, elect representatives, make collective decisions, maintain transparent records and pursue constructive relations with other micronations. It may aspire to wider recognition, but it will not confuse aspiration with achievement.

This is **not a fictional role-playing country**. It is an emerging civic association organised in the form of a micronation. It remains subject to the law of any jurisdiction in which its members live and operate.

### Founding principles

1. **Human dignity and freedom:** Membership is voluntary. No person may be coerced into joining or remaining.
2. **Democratic accountability:** Officeholders are answerable to members under a published constitution.
3. **Peaceful conduct:** No force, trespass, intimidation, impersonation or unlawful occupation.
4. **Rule of law:** Internal decisions have only the force that members voluntarily agree to; Australian and other applicable laws continue to apply.
5. **Transparency and privacy:** Public decisions, private member information.
6. **Integrity:** Titles, territory, finances, membership and recognition must be described truthfully.

## Establishing a functioning micronation

Parristan's state-building work will be assessed against four widely cited criteria from Article 1 of the [1933 Montevideo Convention](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1933v04/d134):

| Criterion | Current evidence | Next milestone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permanent population | Five proposed founding members named by the organiser; individual consent and residence not verified | Confirm each member's participation and establish a separate evidence-based residence record |
| Defined territory | **No territorial claim adopted** | Investigate lawful access to an identifiable site; no land claim without the owner's consent and legal review |
| Government | Draft institutions under development | Ratify a constitution, appoint an interim council, hold an election |
| Capacity for external relations | No recognised state-to-state relations | Establish a correspondence protocol; record any real responses and agreements accurately |

The Montevideo criteria are **not an automatic path to sovereign recognition**. Effective statehood and recognition involve disputed legal and practical questions. A digital repository is a record-keeping tool, not territory.

## Taking part

The first civic milestone is to review and ratify a constitution. Suggested contributions include improving its checks and balances, proposing a practical citizenship system, researching lawful territory and helping establish a transparent public record.

For now, submit **ideas without personal information** through repository issues if you have access. **Do not post home addresses, identification documents, dates of birth or other citizenship evidence into public issues.** A private application process will be established before enrolment opens.

## The distinction that matters

Parristan is a **genuine attempt to organise a micronation**, but it does **not** presently possess legal sovereignty. In Australia, declaring a micronation does not displace Australian law or create a tax exemption. The [Australian Government's Hutt River position](https://uae.embassy.gov.au/abud/huttriver.html) and [academic analysis](https://academic.oup.com/icon/article/19/1/71/6247754) explain that distinction. Parristan will use peaceful, legal processes, not unsupported assertions of immunity.
